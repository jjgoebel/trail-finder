New rider tips
  • Mud = stay home. If your tires leave ruts, turn around. Riding wet trails damages them and gets them closed longer. Check CAMBr Trailbot: green means go, red means closed.
  • Freeze-thaw is sneaky. In late fall and spring, frozen ground is fine early in the morning, but it turns to mud by afternoon.
  • Yield: bikes yield to hikers and horses. On two-way trails, the rider going downhill yields to the one climbing.
  • Bring: helmet, water, a snack, a spare tube or plug kit, a mini pump, and a charged phone.
  • Tire pressure: lower than you'd use on the road (often 20–28 psi). It grips better on roots and gravel.
  • Walk it. Walking a section you're unsure about is normal and smart.

Trail info was hand-checked against CAMBr, the forest preserve districts and community sites. Each card shows the date it was verified. Weather comes from Open-Meteo, and trail lines from OpenStreetMap contributors. The rideability badge is a weather-based guess, not official status.
